Same shopping centre. Different shopping list.

Smaczek is a Polish word with no clean English translation — it means something closer to a tasty little something. A flavour. A treat. The bite of rye bread you finished without noticing.

You walk past us in The Mall: dark wooden shelves, paper bags, the colour of pickled red cabbage in the chiller. Inside is the food a Polish family pantry runs on — fresh rye and sourdough loaves, kabanos and krakowska from the slicer, twaróg, kefir, real żurek, pierogi for the freezer, a counter of cakes nobody can pronounce until they've eaten one.

Codziennie świeże, codziennie z domu. Zapraszamy.

What's in the shop

A short tour, in order of temptation
01

Chleb & bułki Breads & rolls

Dark rye, light wheat, sourdough loaves baked to a Polish recipe — the kind of crust your toaster doesn't know what to do with.

02

Wędliny Smoked & cured meats

Kabanos, krakowska, kiełbasa swojska, smoked ham — sliced fresh from the counter or wrapped to go.

03

Sery & nabiał Cheese & dairy

Twaróg for cheesecake, oscypek when it's around, kefir, śmietana, real butter that tastes like butter.

04

Pierogi & gotowe Dumplings & ready food

Pierogi (ruskie, mięsem, z kapustą), gołąbki, bigos, żurek — fresh and frozen — for the days you don't want to cook.

05

Słodycze Sweets & pastry

Pączki, sernik, makowiec, szarlotka, Prince Polo, krówki, ptasie mleczko — childhood, by the gram.

06

Spiżarnia The pantry

Pickles, sauerkraut, chrzan, ogórki kiszone, barszcz, syrups, jams, kompoty — the long-life shelf every Polish kitchen leans on.
